Definitions
Control - A control is a specific security, privacy, compliance, or operational requirement that an organization claims to have implemented or must satisfy. Examples include requiring multi-factor authentication for privileged access, encrypting sensitive data at rest, maintaining audit logs, or running periodic vulnerability scans. Controls may be written at different levels of detail depending on the source.
Trust Center - A Trust Center is a public or customer-facing webpage or portal where an organization shares information about its security, privacy, compliance, and governance posture. It may include certifications, policies, subprocessors, architecture details, and sometimes explicit or implicit control statements. In this assignment, the Trust Center is one of the sources from which controls must be identified and extracted.
Normalized common control - A normalized common control is a canonical representation of a control intent that can be used to map equivalent or closely related controls across different sources and organizations. Since different organizations may describe the same requirement using different language, structure, or level of specificity, the normalized common control acts as the shared reference point. For example, “Admins must use MFA,” “Privileged access requires a second factor,” and “Administrative accounts are protected with multi-factor authentication” may all map to the same normalized common control.
